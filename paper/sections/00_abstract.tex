\begin{abstract}
Modern Large Language Models remain locked in a costly architectural illusion: the assumption that intelligence demands maintaining an unbounded history of past tokens and verbalizing every intermediate thought into natural language text. In standard Transformers, the strictly positive Softmax attention kernel ($\exp(QK^\top/\sqrt{d}) > 0$) enforces an existential pathology: \textit{an architectural inability to forget}. This forces catastrophic distractor accumulation, context rot, unlearning impossibility, and an $\mathcal{O}(T)$ Key-Value (KV) cache explosion that cripples enterprise serving concurrency. Meanwhile, recent ``deliberative reasoning'' paradigms (e.g., OpenAI o1) incur massive computational waste by generating verbose text chains through full vocabulary projections ($15\times \sim 350\times$ FLOPs), exposing models to cascading token hallucinations.

To break this computational deadlock, we present \textbf{\textsc{Hokie-LM}} (\textsc{NeuroWorld-LM}), a non-Transformer cognitive foundation architecture that unifies continuous-time State-Space Models (SSM) with discrete categorical latent world dynamics. \textsc{Hokie-LM} operates with a strictly invariant $\mathcal{O}(1) = 17.0\text{ KB}$ working memory, eliminating KV caches entirely. We introduce two foundational engines:
(1) The \textbf{Cognitive Active Forgetting Engine (CAFE)}, which partitions continuous states across multi-scale temporal channels ($\boldsymbol{\Omega}$) and executes instantaneous orthogonal subspace nullification ($\mathbf{P}_\perp = \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{V}(\mathbf{V}^\top\mathbf{V})^{-1}\mathbf{V}^\top$). CAFE purges obsolete variables ($99.4\%$ CD-NIAH overwrite accuracy, $+58.8\%p$ over Transformers) and guarantees mathematically provable zero-leakage privacy unlearning ($I(Y_{\mathrm{secret}}; \mathbf{h}^*) \equiv 0$) in $184.3\text{ ms}$ without parameter retraining.
(2) The \textbf{Zero-Token Latent Rollout Planner}, an internalized mental simulation engine that executes deep search trajectories in latent space $(h, z)$ guided by a learned Value Head ($V_\psi$), leaping directly to verified conclusions without emitting redundant text tokens.

Empirical evaluations on an NVIDIA H100 GPU demonstrate that \textsc{Hokie-LM}:
(i) scales to $16\text{,}384$-token associative recall with $81.2\%$ accuracy under a flat $17.0\text{ KB}$ state ($1\text{,}927.5\times$ compression over Transformer KV caches);
(ii) delivers dramatic reasoning leaps on 5-hop deduction (+37.5\%p to $84.4\%$) and GSM8K (+40.0\%p to $70.0\%$) at only $1.25\times$ FLOPs ($11.8\times \sim 12.0\times$ lower compute than Verbal CoT);
(iii) maintains an unyielding $24.7\text{ dB}$ State SNR across 100,000-token streams (vs Transformer decay to $4.0\text{ dB}$) and $96.2\%$ persona fidelity over 100 conversational turns; and
(iv) enables single-GPU serving of 4,096 concurrent deliberative user streams in $17.41\text{ GB}$ VRAM ($963.8\times$ reduction over PagedAttention's $16.38\text{ TB}$).
Our results demonstrate that silent latent world simulation and selective active forgetting establish a superior, constant-memory foundation for autonomous machine intelligence.
\end{abstract}
